How to Convert Temperature, Weight, and Length

Unit Converters · 6 min read

Reading weather forecasts in Toronto, following a baking recipe from London, checking shipping weights in Sydney, or measuring a room in Seattle often requires moving smoothly between metric (SI) and US customary or imperial units. Unlike simple percentage scaling, temperature uses an offset zero point, whereas weight and length scale strictly by fixed international constants.

1. Temperature: Celsius, Fahrenheit & Kelvin

Water freezes at 0 °C (32 °F) and boils at 100 °C (212 °F) at standard atmospheric pressure. Because 100 Celsius degrees span the same gap as 180 Fahrenheit degrees, the ratio is 9/5 (1.8), plus a 32-degree offset:

Temperature Equations: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32   |   °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9   |   K = °C + 273.15

Examples:

  • Oven Baking (180 °C to °F): (180 × 1.8) + 32 = 324 + 32 = 356 °F (about 350 °F in US recipes).
  • Summer Forecast (86 °F to °C): (86 − 32) × 5/9 = 54 × 5/9 = 30 °C.

2. Weight & Mass: Kilograms, Grams, Pounds & Stones

Under the international avoirdupois definition adopted across the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, 1 pound (lb) equals exactly 0.45359237 kilograms (about 453.6 grams), and 1 kilogram equals approximately 2.20462 pounds. In the UK, body weight is also expressed in stones (1 stone = 14 lb ≈ 6.35 kg).

Example (Luggage Limit): A 23 kg airline checked-bag limit converts to 23 × 2.20462 = 50.7 lb.

3. Length & Distance: Meters, Feet, Inches & Miles

Modern length standards anchor directly on the international inch: 1 inch = 2.54 cm (or 25.4 mm) exact. From there, all metric and imperial distances follow clean relationships:

  • 1 foot = 12 inches = 30.48 cm = 0.3048 m
  • 1 yard = 3 feet = 0.9144 m
  • 1 mile = 5,280 feet = 1,609.344 m (≈ 1.609 km)
  • 1 kilometer ≈ 0.62137 miles

Example (Highway Speed & Distance): A 100 km drive equals 100 × 0.62137 = 62.14 miles, while a 6-foot table measures 6 × 0.3048 = 1.829 meters.

Conclusion

Memorizing a few core anchors—1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 kg ≈ 2.205 lb, and water freezing at 0 °C / 32 °F—covers most quick estimates, while digital tools eliminate multi-step rounding errors.
