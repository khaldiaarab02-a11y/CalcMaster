How to Calculate Your BMI

Health & Fitness · 5 min read

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a widely used screening ratio comparing a person's weight to their height squared. Public health agencies across the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Australia use BMI as an accessible starting point for categorizing adult weight ranges—though it works best when combined with other health indicators rather than treated as a standalone verdict.

1. The Metric BMI Formula

If your height is measured in centimeters, divide by 100 first to convert to meters:

Metric Formula: BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [ Height (m) ]²

Metric Example (72 kg, 175 cm):

Convert 175 cm to 1.75 m. Square height: 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625 m². Divide weight: 72 ÷ 3.0625 = 23.5 BMI.

2. The Imperial / US Customary BMI Formula

When working in pounds and feet/inches, convert height into total inches (1 foot = 12 inches) and multiply by the unit conversion factor 703:

Imperial Formula: BMI = [ Weight (lb) ÷ Height (in)² ] × 703

Imperial Example (165 lb, 5 ft 9 in):

Total height is (5 × 12) + 9 = 69 inches. Square height: 69 × 69 = 4,761. Compute (165 ÷ 4,761) × 703 = 24.4 BMI.

3. Standard Adult BMI Categories

BMI Range Standard Adult Classification
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Healthy / Normal Weight
25.0 – 29.9Overweight
30.0 and aboveObesity

4. Key Limitations to Keep in Mind

  • Muscle vs. fat: BMI measures total body mass, not body composition. Strength athletes or muscular individuals may show an elevated BMI despite low body fat.
  • Age and sex differences: Older adults naturally carry less lean muscle mass at a given weight, and standard adult charts do not apply directly to growing children or teens.
  • Fat distribution: Where weight sits matters—waist circumference and waist-to-height ratio provide useful metabolic context alongside BMI.
