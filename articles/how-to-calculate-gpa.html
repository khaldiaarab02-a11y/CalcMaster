How to Calculate Your GPA

Education · 5 min read

Your Grade Point Average (GPA) summarizes academic performance across multiple courses into a single credit-weighted number, most commonly on a 4.0 scale. Because a four-credit lab science course affects your average twice as much as a two-credit elective, calculating GPA is always a weighted average rather than a simple mean.

1. Standard 4.0 Grade Point Scale

While individual colleges and universities sometimes tweak plus/minus values (always check your registrar's official policy), the most widely used North American unweighted scale assigns:

  • A / A+ = 4.0, A− = 3.7
  • B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7
  • C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7
  • D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, F = 0.0

2. The Semester GPA Formula

Multiply each course's numeric grade points by its credit hours to find quality points, sum all quality points, and divide by total attempted credits:

GPA Formula: GPA = Σ (Grade Points × Credit Hours) ÷ Σ Credit Hours

Worked Semester Example (13 Total Credits):

CourseGradePointsCreditsQuality Points
Calculus IA−3.7414.8
English CompositionB+3.339.9
Biology + LabA4.0416.0
Public SpeakingB3.026.0

Total quality points = 14.8 + 9.9 + 16.0 + 6.0 = 46.7. Divide by 13 credits: Semester GPA = 46.7 ÷ 13 = 3.59.

3. Updating Your Cumulative GPA

To combine your latest term with previous semesters, do not simply average two GPAs unless both periods carried identical credit totals. Instead, multiply your prior cumulative GPA by your prior completed credits to recover past quality points, add this term's quality points, and divide by combined credits:

Cumulative GPA: Cumulative GPA = (Prior GPA × Prior Credits + Term Quality Points) ÷ (Prior Credits + Term Credits)

For example, adding the 3.59 (13-credit, 46.7 quality-point) semester above to a prior 3.40 GPA across 30 credits yields (102.0 + 46.7) ÷ 43 = 3.46.

Conclusion

Understanding quality points makes it simple to project honors eligibility, scholarship thresholds, and graduate admissions targets before grades post.
