How to Calculate Percentages: A Complete Guide

Basic & Math · 6 min read

Percentages show up everywhere in daily life—from 15% restaurant tips and 20% VAT receipts to salary raises, exam scores, and mortgage interest rates. Literally translating to "per one hundred," a percentage turns any fraction or comparison into a standardized scale from 0 to 100 so different quantities are effortless to compare side by side.

1. Finding a Percentage of a Number (X% of Y)

The most common task is finding a specific share of a total amount—for example, calculating a discount, tip, or commission. To do this manually, convert the percentage into a decimal by dividing by 100 (moving the decimal point two places left) and multiply by your starting value.

Formula: Part = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Whole

Example (Restaurant Tip & Retail Savings):

  • 18% tip on a $75.00 dinner bill: (18 ÷ 100) × 75 = 0.18 × 75 = $13.50.
  • 25% off a £120 jacket: 0.25 × 120 = £30.00 saved, leaving a sale price of £90.00.

2. Finding What Percent One Number Is of Another

When you already know both the partial score and the total possible amount—such as test marks, survey responses, or budget allocation—divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100.

Formula: Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Example (Exam Score & Household Budget):

  • If you answer 43 out of 50 quiz questions correctly: (43 ÷ 50) × 100 = 86%.
  • If housing costs $1,650 out of a $5,000 monthly income: (1,650 ÷ 5,000) × 100 = 33%.

3. Calculating Percentage Increase and Decrease

Percentage change measures relative growth or decline over time, whether comparing utility bills, investment returns, or website traffic. Always divide the difference by the original (starting) value, never the new value.

Formula: % Change = [ (New Value − Old Value) ÷ |Old Value| ] × 100

Example (Salary Raise vs. Price Drop):

  • Salary increase from $58,000 to $62,640: Difference is +$4,640. Dividing by $58,000 gives 0.08, or a +8.0% raise.
  • Laptop price reduced from $1,000 to $850: Difference is -$150. Dividing by $1,000 gives -15.0%.

4. Why Percentages Are Not Symmetric (The Reverse Trap)

A frequent mistake is assuming that increasing a number by 20% and then decreasing it by 20% brings you back to where you started. Because the second percentage applies to a larger base, the drop removes more than the initial gain:

Start with 100 → add 20% to reach 120 → subtract 20% of 120 (24) → you land at 96, which is 4% lower than your starting point.

To reverse a markup or sales tax added earlier, divide by 1 + rate instead of subtracting the percentage directly. For instance, removing 10% GST from a $110 AUD receipt means dividing 110 ÷ 1.10 = $100 pre-tax, rather than taking 10% off $110 ($99).

5. Quick Mental Math Shortcuts

  • 10% rule: Move the decimal point one digit left (10% of 240 is 24). Halve that for 5% (12), or double it for 20% (48).
  • 1% rule: Move the decimal point two digits left (1% of 350 is 3.5), then multiply by your target percentage.
  • Swap symmetry: X% of Y always equals Y% of X. For example, 16% of 50 is identical to 50% of 16 = 8!

Conclusion

Understanding which value serves as the denominator—the whole total or the original starting amount—makes every percentage calculation straightforward.
