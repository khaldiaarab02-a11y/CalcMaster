Compound Interest Formula

For a lump-sum principal P compounded n times per year at nominal annual rate r over t years:

A = P(1 + r / n)^(n × t)  |  APY = (1 + r / n)^n − 1

When recurring monthly deposits are included, CalcMaster converts your selected compounding frequency into its equivalent effective monthly rate i_m = (1 + r / n)^(n / 12) − 1 and accumulates end-of-month deposits over all 12 × t months.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Select your currency symbol and enter an initial deposit (or 0 if starting purely from monthly contributions).
  2. Specify your recurring monthly contribution, annual nominal rate (APR), and duration in whole years.
  3. Choose how frequently interest compounds (Daily, Monthly, Quarterly, Semi-Annually, or Annually).
  4. Click Calculate to inspect your projected balance, APY, and year-by-year growth schedule.

Practical Examples

1. Investing With Monthly Deposits: Starting with $10,000 and adding $300/month for 15 years at 7% annual rate (monthly compounding) contributes $64,000.00 out of pocket, earns $59,578.16 in compound growth, and reaches $123,578.16 (7.23% APY).

2. Daily Compounding Lump Sum: Placing $5,000 with $0/month additions at 5% compounded daily (365/yr) for 10 years grows to $8,243.32 (5.13% APY), earning $3,243.32 in interest.

Why Compounding Frequency Matters

More frequent compounding credits interest earlier so future interest earns returns sooner. At a 7% nominal rate, annual compounding yields 7.00% APY, monthly compounding yields 7.23% APY, and daily compounding yields 7.25% APY.

Frequently Asked Questions

APR is the stated nominal annual interest rate without accounting for intra-year compounding. APY (Effective Annual Yield) measures the actual percentage growth over one full year once compounding frequency is factored in.
Deposits are modeled at the end of each month (ordinary annuity timing), which is the standard conservative baseline for payroll-deducted savings plans.
Financial Assumptions & Disclaimer: Projections assume a constant nominal rate of return, end-of-month contributions, and exclude income taxes, inflation, and advisory/account fees. Real investment returns fluctuate and involve risk; this tool is for educational planning only and does not constitute personalized investment advice.
