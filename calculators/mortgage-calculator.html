How Mortgage Payments Are Calculated

A fixed-rate mortgage amortizes your financed principal (Home Price minus Down Payment) into equal periodic Principal & Interest (P&I) payments according to your chosen payment frequency (12, 24, 26, or 52 payments per year), plus optional annual property tax, homeowners insurance, and HOA escrow:

Payment = [ L × r(1 + r)^N / ((1 + r)^N − 1) ] + (Tax + Insurance + 12×HOA) / k

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Select your currency, enter the home purchase price, and specify your down payment as a percentage or dollar amount.
  2. Enter the annual interest rate (APR), loan duration in years, and desired Payment Frequency (Monthly, Semi-Monthly, Bi-Weekly, or Weekly).
  3. Add annual property taxes, homeowners insurance, and monthly HOA dues if applicable, then click Calculate.

Practical Examples

1. Standard 30-Year Monthly Mortgage: On a $400,000 home with 20% down ($80,000), the financed loan is $320,000. At 6.25% APR over 30 years paid monthly, P&I is $1,970.30/mo. Adding $4,200/yr property tax ($350/mo) and $1,500/yr insurance ($125/mo) gives $2,445.30/mo.

2. Comparing Bi-Weekly Frequency: Switching the same 30-year mortgage to Bi-Weekly (26 payments/yr) recalculates periodic P&I and escrow across 780 bi-weekly installments, helping align mortgage outflows with bi-weekly paychecks.

Frequently Asked Questions
