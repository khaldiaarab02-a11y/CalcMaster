Exact Temperature Conversion Formulas

Unlike length or weight, Celsius and Fahrenheit have an offset zero point (0 °C equals 32 °F) in addition to a 9/5 scale ratio:

°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32  |  °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9  |  K = °C + 273.15

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Enter any temperature reading at or above absolute zero (-273.15 °C / -459.67 °F / 0 K).
  2. Choose your source scale under From Scale and destination scale under To Scale.
  3. View the main converted readout plus synchronized Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin cards simultaneously.

Practical Examples

1. Boiling Point of Water (100 °C to Fahrenheit & Kelvin): (100 × 9/5) + 32 produces 212 °F and 373.15 K at standard atmospheric pressure.

2. Room Temperature (68 °F to Celsius): (68 − 32) × 5/9 equals 20 °C and 293.15 K. Meanwhile, -40 °C equals -40 °F—the exact intersection of both scales.

Frequently Asked Questions

Absolute zero (0 K, equal to -273.15 °C and -459.67 °F) is the lowest physically attainable thermodynamic temperature.
Under International System (SI) conventions, Kelvin is written simply as K (e.g., 293.15 K) without a degree sign.
Science Note: Standard linear thermodynamic scale relationships are applied. Cooking and industrial equipment calibration tolerances may vary slightly from theoretical values.
