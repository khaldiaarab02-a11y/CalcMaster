Simple Interest Formula

Simple interest grows linearly because interest is charged or earned strictly on the original principal balance rather than previously accrued interest:

I = P × r × t  |  Total Amount A = P(1 + r × t)
  • I = Total interest accrued
  • P = Principal amount
  • r = Annual interest rate expressed as a decimal (e.g., 4.5% = 0.045)
  • t = Time in years (Months ÷ 12 or Days ÷ 365)

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Choose your preferred display currency (currency selection changes formatting symbols only).
  2. Enter the starting principal amount and the annual nominal interest rate.
  3. Enter the duration and choose whether the number represents years, months, or calendar days (using a 365-day convention).
  4. Click Calculate to review total interest, final payoff/maturity value, and monthly average interest.

Practical Examples

1. Three-Year Promissory Note: Lending $5,000 at 4.5% annual simple interest for 3 years earns I = 5,000 × 0.045 × 3 = $675.00, producing a final maturity amount of $5,675.00 ($18.75/month average).

2. 90-Day Short-Term Treasury / Bridge Note: Investing $12,000 at 6% for 90 days converts time to 90 / 365 = 0.24658 years, yielding I = $177.53 and a total payout of $12,177.53.

Simple vs. Compound Interest

Simple interest is commonly used for short-term personal notes, auto finance charges calculated on daily simple interest schedules, and single-period certificates. For savings accounts, index investments, and credit cards where unpaid or earned interest compounds over multiple periods, use the Compound Interest Calculator instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

When "Days" is selected, CalcMaster converts days to years using a standard 365-day actual-year divisor (days / 365). Some commercial banks use a 360-day year convention for money-market quotes.
For any positive interest rate and duration longer than one compounding period, simple interest yields less total interest than compound interest because compound interest earns return on previously accumulated interest.
