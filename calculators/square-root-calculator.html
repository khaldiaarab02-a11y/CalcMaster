Square Root & N-th Root Formula

Taking the n-th root of a number x is the inverse operation of raising a value to the n-th power. Mathematically, any radical can be written as a fractional exponent:

√x = x^(1/2)  |  ∛x = x^(1/3)  |  ⁿ√x = x^(1/n)

For positive even roots (such as n = 2 or n = 4), there are two real roots: a positive principal root and its negative opposite (since (−12)² = 144 as well). Odd roots (such as cube roots) yield a single real root that preserves the sign of the original number.

Practical Examples

1. Square Room Diagonal & Side Length: If a square garden plot covers 144 sq ft, each side measures √144 = 12 ft (a perfect square, with algebraic solutions ±12).

2. Negative Cube Root: Suppose a cubic change scales by −27. Selecting Cube Root computes ∛(−27) = −3 because (−3)³ = −27.

3. Compound Annual Growth (4th Root): An investment growing by a factor of 1.4641 over 4 years has an annual growth multiplier of 1.4641^(1/4) = 1.10, or 10% per year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Multiplying any real number by itself produces zero or a positive result, so no real number squares to a negative value. Negative radicands require imaginary or complex numbers (using i = √−1).
A perfect square is an integer formed by squaring a whole number (such as 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 144). Its square root is an exact integer without non-zero decimals.
